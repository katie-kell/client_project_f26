Preparing for Your Experience

Start With a Strong Foundation

Before jumping into your project, take some time to understand what you are getting into. Learn about your partner, think about what you want to get out of the experience, and figure out what your team needs to be successful.

Why Preparation Matters

Engaged learning is different from a normal class project. You are working with real people and organizations, which means your work can have an impact beyond your final grade.

It can be tempting to jump straight into ideas and solutions, but first make sure you actually understand the problem. Learning about your partner, your users, and the context behind the project can save your team a lot of confusion later.

Come In Ready to Learn

You might have skills that are useful to your partner, but that does not mean you already know the best solution. Your partner understands their organization and community in ways that you do not.

Ask questions, listen, and be open to changing your ideas. Think about how your own experiences and assumptions might affect the way you approach the project.

What Should You Know Before Starting?

  • Know the context: Learn about the organization, community, and people your project might affect.
  • Know the expectations: Understand important deadlines, responsibilities, commitments, and expected outcomes.
  • Know your team: Figure out what everyone is good at, what they want to learn, and where your team might need more support.
  • Know the problem: Understand what your partner actually needs before deciding what the solution should be.

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • What do I want to learn from this experience?
  • Who will actually be affected by our work?
  • What assumptions am I already making about the project?
  • What does my team already know how to do?
  • What are we missing?
  • How involved will our partner be in project decisions?
  • What would a successful project look like for everyone involved?

A Simple Way to Prepare

  1. Reflect: Think about the experiences and assumptions you are bringing into the project.
  2. Research: Learn about your partner, their organization, their users, and the context surrounding the project.
  3. Set goals: Decide what you personally want to learn or get better at during the experience.
  4. Check your team's skills: Figure out what your team can already do and what you may need to learn along the way.
  5. Write down your questions: Keep track of anything you still need to ask your partner before deciding on the scope or solution.

Resources to Help You Prepare

  • Social Identity Snapshot - Reflect on your identity and how your experiences might shape the way you approach your work.
  • "Check Yourself" Community Engagement Checklist - Check some of your assumptions before working with a community or partner.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation - Understand different ways communities can be involved in decisions.
  • Project Scoping Handout & Terms - Use this to turn your partner's needs into a realistic project scope.
  • Student Organization Officer Guide and Project Manager Guide - Learn more about roles and expectations for student leaders.
  • Project Manager Commitment Form - Review the responsibilities that come with leading a project.
  • Project Management Worksheet - Start organizing your scope, goals, team roles, and timeline.

Think You're Ready?

You do not need to have everything figured out before the project starts. You should have a good understanding of your partner, your team, and what you still need to learn.

Next, it is time to turn that preparation into an actual plan.
